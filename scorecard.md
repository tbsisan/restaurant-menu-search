# Scorecard: Restaurant Menu Search

## Minimal incubator rubric

### 1. What value does this create?
Likely: It helps users find specific dishes nearby faster than checking many menus manually. Tentative additional value: it may let users order pickup at normal in-store prices rather than marked-up marketplace prices.

### 2. Who wants this, and how badly?
Tentative: The strongest likely users are people with specific cravings, food explorers, and price-sensitive pickup customers. Need evidence on frequency and pain intensity; the raw concept does not include user interviews, demand signals, or usage data.

### 3. Why now?
Tentative: AI phone agents and menu parsing/search are more plausible now than before. However, the raw concept does not establish why restaurants, users, or data access conditions make this especially timely.

### 4. What’s the smallest sharp MVP?
Likely: A local pilot for one neighborhood or cuisine cluster with dish-level menu indexing, search results, and human-in-the-loop phone pickup ordering. This would test demand, menu accuracy, and ordering reliability before building broad coverage.

### 5. Why would this spread?
Tentative: It could spread through memorable craving-based use cases, such as "find the best nearby chicken parm" or "who has soup dumplings right now?" Word of mouth depends on reliability and whether users save money or discover better options.

### 6. Why might it fail?
Likely: It could fail because menu data gets stale, AI phone calls are unreliable, restaurants dislike automated ordering, price guarantees are hard, and the product may be squeezed between existing search, maps, delivery apps, and restaurant-owned ordering.

### 7. What’s the weird / non-obvious edge?
Likely: Dish-first search plus AI phone ordering at normal in-store prices. The product is less about "browse restaurants" and more about "satisfy this exact craving without paying marketplace tax."

### 8. What should happen next?
Run a critique pass. The concept is clear enough to stress-test, and the biggest current need is identifying fatal versus fixable risks around data, ordering, payments, restaurant acceptance, and user demand.

## What would most improve this idea?
