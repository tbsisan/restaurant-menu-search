## Important: how to read this run

This was a full three-round council run:

- eight Stage-1 reviews per round;
- eight anonymized Stage-2 peer rankings per round;
- a MiniMax M3 Stage-3 synthesis per round;
- three configured rounds executed; `auto_converge` was disabled and the saved
  run says `converged: false`.

Round 1 is the closest thing to an independent blind review. Rounds 2 and 3
are deliberative refinements: models receive the prior synthesis and peer
feedback under the old application's ordinary multi-round flow.

### Do not confuse Stage 4 with the review input

The conversation also contains `metadata.stage4`, a 21k-character rewrite by
the chairman of the original review prompt. It is the source of the `[NEW]` and
`[REVISED]` material seen in the saved rewritten text. For this kind of code
review, that rewrite is not useful evidence of what the reviewers discovered;
it is a post-review editorial artifact. It should not be treated as the prompt
that guided the original Stage-1 reviews, and it should not be used as proof

## What the Stage-3 verdict concluded

The chairman judged the spike's overall architectural route to be sound:

- capture `itemPage` rather than depend on incomplete JSON-LD for modifiers;
- collect virtualized cards while scrolling;
- checkpoint item captures;
- recover from lost tabs;
- keep the spike concrete rather than introducing a broad framework.

Its main judgment was that the emitted harvest artifact is not yet trustworthy
as “complete,” because several quiet failures can yield a clean-looking output.

The verdict's primary issue cluster was:

1. `complete=True` is written at the end even after failures or an empty
   collection.
2. Repeated item IDs can overwrite category information rather than preserve
   all observed memberships.
3. Discovery completion relies on a brittle scrolling heuristic and does not
   clearly record why it stopped.
4. Item-page fetching conflates different failure classes into an opaque
   failure, limiting diagnosis and appropriate retry behavior.
5. Recovery/resume lacks enough identity checking to rule out the wrong store
   or an incompatible old checkpoint.
6. Discovery work is less durable than per-item work; browser loss during
   scrolling can discard it.
7. Diagnostic probing and real collection may select different card selectors.

The verdict also named state cleanup, resume-record duplication, recorder
timing, and page-readiness/empty-menu detection as important secondary areas.

## Assessment of the verdict

### Strongest, code-grounded directions

These are the most credible places to start when inspecting the real spike:

- Make the harvest's completeness claim evidence-based and diagnosable.
- Preserve category membership rather than allowing virtualized observation
  order to decide an item's sole category.
- Expose discovery outcome separately from item-fetch outcome.
- Preserve enough failure information to tell a transient browser/network
  problem from an invalid or changed GraphQL payload.
- Verify recovery and resume identity before continuing a capture.

These align with the system's stated product constraint: an incomplete capture
must never be converted into a deceptively successful final artifact.

### Findings that require an operational check before code changes

The council itself identified some claims as conditional on Camofox or live
DoorDash behavior. Do not promote these from hypotheses to defects without a
small reproducible check:

- whether Camofox `/evaluate` awaits JavaScript Promises;
- whether its `/click` endpoint supports Playwright-style `:has-text()`;
- whether the actual menu scroll container is `window` or a nested element;
- whether a given apparent scroll stall is a terminal page state or delayed
  hydration;
- whether response identity fields are consistently present in real
  `itemPage` payloads.

### Useful peer-review dissent

The Stage-2 reviewers caught several reasons not to blindly merge every idea:

- Do not use JSON-LD counts as a simple completeness oracle; the known
  “Most Ordered” duplication makes naive count comparisons unsafe.
- Retain a bounded scroll safety cap; replace silent success at the cap with
  an observable incomplete outcome rather than removing the cap entirely.
- Avoid generic browser/pacer/retry frameworks while this remains one spike.
- Keep the number of prioritized changes constrained; several otherwise good
  Stage-1 reviews exceeded the requested eight-action limit or diluted the
  high-signal issues with minor observations.

## Recommended next session approach

1. Locate and inspect the real spike plus `parse_doordash_itempage.py`; the
   embedded copy in the conversation is evidence, not the authoritative file.
2. Write down the current harvest-file contract before changing it.
3. Validate the operational hypotheses above on permitted live pages or
   reproducible fixtures.
4. Prioritize artifact honesty and observability before convenience,
   performance, or abstraction work.
5. Keep the direct `itemPage` path and nested modifier tree intact. Do not
   replace it with JSON-LD, and do not add access-control or CAPTCHA evasion.

## Completion bar for the spike

A reviewer should be able to inspect one saved harvest artifact, without
reading terminal logs, and tell:

- which store/request produced it;
- whether discovery completed and why it stopped;
- what items/categories were observed;
- which item captures succeeded, failed, retried, or were intentionally
  limited;
- whether successful item payloads were structurally usable;
- why the artifact is, or is not, entitled to claim completeness.
